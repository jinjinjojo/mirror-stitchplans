Sizing

Size it to a body

Measure the body, choose the ease, and turn both into stitches and rows at your own gauge. A starting point for your own design, or a check on a pattern that doesn't fit.

You'll need a tape measure, your stitch and row gauge

Sweater

Sleeves get 1 in (2.5 cm) of ease at the cuff and 2 in (5 cm) at the top.

Hat, in the round

Hats stretch, so their ease is negative: enter it with a minus sign.

Anything else

A sock, a mitten, a cowl: one measurement and its ease.

How much ease?

FitEase on the chestWears like
Very close0 or a little lessA fitted rib top
Close1 to 2 in (2.5 to 5 cm)A slim jumper
Classic2 to 4 in (5 to 10 cm)An everyday sweater
Loose4 to 6 in (10 to 15 cm)Room for a shirt underneath
Oversized6 in (15 cm) or moreA deliberately big sweater

The quickest honest check is to measure a garment you already like wearing, lay flat, and compare it with your chest. The difference is the ease you prefer. Estimated These ranges are common guidance, not a rule: a stiff cotton wants more ease than a springy wool.

How to measure

  • Chest: around the fullest part, tape level under the arms, one finger's slack.
  • Upper arm: around the fullest part, arm relaxed.
  • Hem to underarm: from where you want the sweater to end, up to about 1 in (2.5 cm) below the armpit.
  • Cuff to underarm: along the inside of a slightly bent arm.
  • Head: around the widest part, just above the ears and across the forehead.

Every figure here depends on your gauge. If you haven't swatched, read your swatch first; then count the yarn.

Formulas

Stitches = (measurement + ease) × stitches per unit, moved to the nearest count that fits your stitch pattern. Rows = length × rows per unit. A hat's cast-on is a multiple of 8 and of the rib, so 8 decreases every other round take it down to 8 stitches. Verified Every build checks these formulas with worked examples.
