Shaping

Shaping over several rows

Sleeves, raglans and tapers change stitches over many rows, not one. Give it the counts and the rows you have, and it writes the shaping line, row by row, the way a pattern would.

You'll need your start and end stitch counts, your row gauge

Rows = length × rows per inch. Read your row gauge on the gauge page.

How the line is worked out

  • Shaping rows are the stitches to change divided by how many each row changes. Going from 60 to 84 at 2 a row takes 12 shaping rows.
  • When it doesn't divide evenly the line uses two intervals, like “every 6th row 8 times, then every 8th row 3 times”, and the faster ones come first. Swap them if your pattern wants the shaping late, as a sleeve cap does.
  • Right-side rows only keeps every interval even, so shaping never lands on a purl row. Row 1 counts as a right-side row.
  • Plain rows at the end are what's left over: work them straight, before the next part of the pattern.

For one row of shaping, use increase or decrease evenly. Don't have the counts yet? Size the sleeve first.

A worked sleeve

A sleeve goes from 38 stitches at the cuff to 70 at the top over 126 rows, two stitches at a time on right-side rows. That's 16 shaping rows in 63 pairs of rows: 3 pairs each, with 15 pairs over. The line comes out as every 6th row once, then every 8th row 15 times, which uses all 126 rows. Try those numbers.

How we check it

The written schedule is replayed on every build across thousands of start counts, end counts and row totals: it must end on the promised count, stay inside the rows you gave it, and land on right-side rows when asked. Verified Every build checks these formulas with worked examples.
