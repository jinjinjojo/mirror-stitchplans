Gauge, in short

Gauge (tension in the UK) is how many stitches and rows fit in 4 inches or 10 centimetres. Half a stitch an inch sounds small, but on a 40-inch sweater at 5 stitches an inch it's 4 inches of difference. Swatch with the needles and yarn you'll use, wash and dry it the way you'll treat the finished piece, then measure in the middle. Stitches per 4 inches and per 10 cm are close enough to swap (10 cm is 3.94 inches).

Knitting to a body rather than a width? Size a sweater or hat from your measurements. Gauge also sets how much yarn you need: weigh your swatch to work it out. Don't know which needle to start with? See the yarn weight chart.

Formulas

Stitches per 4 in = stitches counted ÷ width × 4 (× 10 for cm). Cast-on = width × stitches per unit, moved to the nearest count that is a multiple of your pattern repeat plus the extra stitches. Resized count = pattern count × your gauge ÷ pattern gauge. Verified Every build checks these formulas with worked examples.
