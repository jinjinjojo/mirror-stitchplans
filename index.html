The rest of the pattern

  1. Find your standard sizeA women's medium chest is 36 to 38 in: 180 to 190 stitches at 20 sts per 4 in.
  2. Look up a head, foot or handAn adult head is 21 to 23 in around: 105 to 115 stitches at 20 sts per 4 in.
  3. Shape a sleeveIncrease 2 sts every 6th row 12 times.
  4. Size it to a bodyA 38 in chest plus 4 in of ease at 20 sts: 208 around.
  5. Read your swatch22 sts over 4 in is 5.5 stitches an inch.
  6. Count the yarnWeigh a swatch, scale it up by area, round up to whole balls.
  7. Swap US and UK termsA US dc becomes a UK tr, once, never twice.

How the row is spread

  • Flat rows split the leftover gap between both ends, so the first and last increase sit away from the edge and seams stay neat: 60 stitches + 6 is K5, M1, [k10, M1] 5 times, k5.
  • In the round every gap is the same and the end of the round counts as one: [K10, M1] 6 times.
  • When it doesn't divide evenly, the longer and shorter gaps alternate through the row instead of bunching at one end, so the fabric doesn't pull.
  • The method changes the maths. M1 and yarn overs are made between stitches and use none up. Kfb and “2 sc in next” are worked into a stitch, so each one uses a stitch. Decreases work two stitches together. Calculators that treat them alike give rows that end on the wrong count.

How we check it

The written row is replayed stitch by stitch to make sure it uses exactly the stitches you have and ends on the count promised.
